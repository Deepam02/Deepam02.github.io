How I think

Built to think
like a founder.
Wired to ship
like an engineer.

Most engineers see a ticket. I see a decision. Every system I've built started with a conversation about whether to build it at all. That's not seniority — that's just the only way to build things that survive contact with reality.

"The architecture decision I'm most proud of isn't the one I made — it's the one I talked us out of. We saved three weeks by not building the wrong abstraction."
— Deepam, on a SWARM refactor that didn't happen
01 —
Full-stack ownership
Terraform to React. Infrastructure to conversation design. I don't hand things off to "the infra person" — at a 4-person startup, I am the infra person, the AI engineer, and occasionally the product manager.
02 —
Speed with judgment
Moving fast is worthless if it breaks. I ship quickly, document properly, and design for the person who has to understand this system six months from now — because that person is usually me.
03 —
Integration-first thinking
LLMs in notebooks aren't products. I've wired agents to Jira, Outlook, WhatsApp, and live alarm systems — with Svix signature verification, atomic state transitions, and real error handling. Completely. Not theoretically.
04 —
Founder-adjacent thinking
I've worked next to product decisions, not just below them. I know the difference between what a founder needs to hear and what sounds technically impressive. That distinction matters more than most engineers admit.
